## If you only check ChatGPT, you're only seeing half the market

Some agents have noticed something odd: ask Perplexity "who are the recommended Chinese-speaking realtors in Ottawa" and your name is on the list; ask ChatGPT the same question and you are nowhere. The first reaction is relief: at least one engine mentions me.

That relief is dangerous. **Your clients don't all use one AI.** Some use ChatGPT, some use Gemini, some use Perplexity, and some read Google AI Mode directly. If you only check yourself on ChatGPT, you're only seeing half the market — and you have no idea whether you're invisible in the other half.

Different shortlists across engines are normal, not a malfunction. The reason lies in a fundamental difference — what material each engine reads.

## The four engines don't read the same pile of material

ChatGPT's search tracks Bing's results closely: a small 2025 study found over 87% of ChatGPT search citations matched Bing's top 20 results (Seer Interactive, limited sample). Gemini and Google AI Mode build on Google's own index. Perplexity uses its own crawler, fetching and citing sources in real time. Different material pools produce different shortlists.

**Measured data confirms this fragmentation.** Cheers' baseline study of 34,742 AI answers found that, for the same city and question in the same week, 77% of businesses named appeared in only one of the four engines. Goodjuju's study of property managers across 30 US cities found a similar pattern: 70% of named companies appeared in only one engine, and only 22 companies were named by all four. Wrenfield's October 2026 test of 48 local marketing agencies found 31% were never named by any engine; by engine, ChatGPT named 50% of the group, while Google AI Mode named only 23%.

Note the limits of these studies: their samples are US home services, property management, and local marketing agencies — not Ottawa realtors. They cannot tell you how you personally perform on any engine, but they establish a structural fact: **disagreement between engines is the norm, not something targeted at an individual.**

**There is another often-missed difference: the same engine weighs material differently depending on the question.** Cheers found that 84% of ChatGPT's answers cited at least one directory, review platform, or "best of" list, while 92.5% of Google AI Mode's answers cited the business's own website. In other words, ChatGPT leans more on "what others say about you," while Google AI Mode leans more on "what your own website says." The same business performing differently under those two weightings is entirely normal.

## What this means for your business

**Judging yourself by one engine will mislead you.** Appearing in Perplexity but not ChatGPT does not mean you are "getting worse"; appearing in ChatGPT does not mean you are visible to every buyer. Buyers use different engines, so your visibility is the overall picture across engines — not a single result in a single chat box.

**Do not pay for a separate playbook "for one engine."** Since engines draw on different material pools and weightings, single-engine "special optimization" rarely survives the next engine adjustment. The steadier approach is to keep the public facts about you consistent and verifiable wherever engines can read them: identity, service areas, who you serve, and client reviews. Those basic facts are useful in every engine's material pool.

**Being mentioned does not mean better, and being absent does not mean worse.** Engine shortlists reflect the distribution of public material, not a ranking of service quality. The fact that engines disagree with each other is itself proof that no single shortlist is "the standard answer."
