You're a realtor in Toronto working with Chinese-speaking buyers. This week you heard the news: ChatGPT has started showing ads.

The thought that probably followed was something like this — so why bother with GEO? Why not just buy an ad and get ChatGPT to recommend me in the answer?

Here's the short answer: **an ad buys the spot below the answer. It doesn't buy the answer itself.**

## What the ads actually look like

Let's get the facts straight before anyone spends money on this:

ChatGPT ads began testing in the United States on February 9, 2026, and expanded to Canada in March. They only appear to logged-in adult users on the Free and Go plans — Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Education accounts never see them at all.

The ad sits below the answer, labelled Sponsored. And OpenAI has said it plainly: advertisers cannot change the answer itself.

So what your money buys is a position underneath the answer. Who the answer names and recommends — that part is not for sale.

## Buyers trust the answer first

When a buyer asks ChatGPT "who is a good Chinese-speaking realtor in Toronto," the first thing they read is the names in the answer. The ad is further down, waiting for them to scroll and click.

And then there's the other half of the audience: users on Plus, Pro and business accounts never see your ad at all. All they ever see is the answer.

An ad is rented space — it's there while you pay, and gone the day you stop. A recommendation inside the answer is earned space: ask again in different words and it will often still be there — but every answer is a fresh draw, so nobody can promise it will be there every single time.

Think about your own buyers. Someone about to buy in Markham has already compared options online before they ever ask an AI. They aren't looking for an ad slot. They're looking for a name they can trust. An ad can get them to notice you. The answer is what gets them to believe you. Those are two different things.

## Why trying it once yourself doesn't tell you much

You may have already opened ChatGPT and tried it: ask once, see whether it recommends you.

But what you see depends on your own plan. A buyer on a different plan sees a different page — the ads you see, they may never see; the version without ads that you never see, they may see every day.

One try from your own account only shows you your own side of it. Which entry point buyers come in through, whose name they see first in the answer, and where yours drops off — you can't see all of that from inside your own account.

That's what a visibility check is for: not asking the AI once, but standing where the buyer stands, asking several times and several ways, and finding out who keeps getting mentioned — and where you're falling behind.
