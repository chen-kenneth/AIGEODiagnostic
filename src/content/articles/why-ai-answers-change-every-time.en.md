## A misjudgment that can cost you business

On Monday, you checked for yourself: you asked ChatGPT "who are good Chinese-speaking realtors in Ottawa," and your name was on the list. You relaxed.

On Wednesday, you checked again: you were gone. You started to worry — is something wrong with my website? Has some engine "demoted" me? Should I change something, fast?

Both reactions can cost you money. The first makes you overhaul things over a single fluctuation. The second — assuming you're "on the list" because you appeared once — is more dangerous, because it leaves you unaware when you're actually absent.

Here is both a reassurance and a warning: **this kind of fluctuation is normal, not something wrong with you specifically.** On OpenAI's developer forum, someone asked whether it is normal for the same prompt to produce clearly different results across runs. The answer: completely normal. But "normal" does not mean "ignore it" — the fluctuation itself is exactly why you cannot judge your own situation by feel.

## Why the answer changes every time

Three reasons stack up.

**First, AI doesn't look things up — it writes fresh each time.** Traditional search ranks from a relatively stable index, so the same query returns roughly stable results over a short period. A conversational AI generates its answer word by word, each time picking from several likely options by probability. One different word early on sends the whole passage in another direction. So the same question run twice can differ in wording, names, and order. That is not a malfunction — it is how this kind of model works.

**Second, the material it retrieves changes each time.** For local recommendation questions, AI usually runs a live web search first, then writes its answer from the pages it found. And the set of pages it finds can change every time: new content gets published, old pages get updated, and the search engine's own ranking shifts. Research has noted that 40%–60% of the domains cited in AI answers change within a month (AirOps data, via AI Advisors' summary). When the source material changes, the list follows.

**Third, the asker and the wording change too.** Location, conversation context, and the exact phrasing of the question all affect the answer. A 2026 University of Maryland study found that rephrasing the same question in equivalent ways produced contradictory answers in up to 47% of question groups (via Rauno's summary). In other words, what looks like "the same question" from you and from a client may already be a different question to the AI.

How big is this fluctuation? A few cross-industry measurements give a sense of scale — with an important boundary: none of these samples are Ottawa realtors, so they cannot be applied directly to your market. They only show the magnitude. Texta's test of 10,000 identical queries found 34% of answers differed substantially, with brand-mention changes accounting for 21.4%. Cheers' baseline study of 34,742 AI answers found that when the same question was asked a week later, ChatGPT repeated the same set of businesses only 58% of the time. A January 2026 SparkToro and Gumshoe study (about 600 volunteers, 2,961 runs) confirmed that lists systematically float across runs.

## What this means for your business

**Checking for yourself once shows you only one moment, one engine, one phrasing.** But when clients ask AI, the moment, the engine, and the phrasing are all different from yours. A conclusion drawn from one search — "I'm there" or "I'm not there" — may have nothing to do with what your clients actually see. Making decisions from that kind of spot check is gambling on volatility.

**A single result proves nothing; the trend does.** Appearing once does not mean you have "made the list." Disappearing once does not mean something broke. What is worth watching is whether, over a period of time and across engines, you appear steadily — and whether that is improving or worsening.

**Don't trust any promise of "appearing every time."** Since answers are generated probabilistically, nobody can guarantee a name appears in every answer. Any service that writes "recommended every time" into its promise deserves skepticism.

**Being mentioned correlates with business — it is not the cause of it.** An agent who is often mentioned by AI and an agent whose deals come from AI are two different things. When you see a list change, don't rush to rebuild your website or switch strategy — first get a clear picture of your real presence.
