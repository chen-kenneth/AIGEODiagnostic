## Seeing yourself once proves very little

Seeing yourself now doesn't mean you'll appear every time.

Ask the same question several times and the list changes. In a study published by [SparkToro and Gumshoe](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/) in January 2026, 600 volunteers ran 12 questions across ChatGPT, Claude and Google's AI a combined 2,961 times — and the same list of brands came back twice less than 1 time in 100.

An AI ranking is not a position in a single screenshot. It is several things together, measured over many answers: how often AI mentions you, whether it clearly recommends you, whether it puts you on its shortlist, whether it ranks you in the top three, whether it mentions you across different AIs, and whether the facts it gives about you are correct.

## Standing still is not standing still

Even if you change nothing, what AI answers contain keeps rotating. [SISTRIX](https://www.sistrix.com/blog/ai-citation-drift-how-stable-are-sources-in-ai-search-results/)'s May 2026 study (82,619 questions over 17 weeks) found that of the domains ChatGPT cited each week, 74% were new — domains that hadn't appeared the week before. To be clear, that is rotation in cited sources, not a 74% drop in brand recommendation rates.

What these numbers show is that AI visibility fluctuates. Not because you did anything wrong — the answers change on their own. That is not proof that stopping maintenance will make you drop. No such study exists.

But a few things don't stop just because you did: listings expire in days, prices and status change constantly, competitors keep collecting fresh reviews; citations shift when models update too. When AI answers a question, it may draw on what it learned in training, or it may look things up live — both happen. The part that is looked up live can come back different every time.

## What maintenance is: no need to push hard, but you do need to hold the line

This article is not asking you to run a big campaign every month. Your AI ranking doesn't need aggressive work to climb higher — **it needs maintenance**.

Maintenance is two things: continued monitoring — watching how you're cited across different AI platforms, which can help spot changes worth paying attention to in time; and light updates — so key facts are checked and corrected as soon as they go out of date.

AI algorithms keep changing, and what gets cited changes with them — so how GEO is done has to change too. Maintenance isn't repeating the same playbook; it's adjusting priorities based on monitoring results, platform changes, and changes to your brand information.

One more thing: if you stop updating, your competitors won't. Continued monitoring can help spot changes worth paying attention to in time, so you can assess whether anything needs to change.

If your ranking has been stable at the top for a while, the scope of service can be reassessed based on your actual situation, including whether a monitoring-led service would fit. Worth a conversation.

Nobody has measured what happens to an Ottawa or Toronto realtor who stops GEO, and that should be said plainly. What is certain: AI visibility fluctuates; only continued monitoring tells you whether you're holding or slipping; and key facts only stay correct if someone maintains them.
