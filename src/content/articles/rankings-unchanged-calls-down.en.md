You check your rankings every month. You're still on page one of Google, still in the top three, and your website traffic hasn't collapsed. But the phone rings less than it used to, and showing appointments are down too.

You assume the market cooled off, or it's a slow season. There is one possibility you haven't counted: buyers changed entrances.

They asked the AI first. It handed them a shortlist with phone numbers and hours right there, and they called the first name on it — which wasn't yours. And you never find out that call was once yours to win.

## Why your reports show nothing wrong

Ranking and traffic dashboards measure the old funnel: someone searches, someone clicks, someone reaches your website, someone calls.

An AI answer doesn't go through your website. No click means no visit recorded; no visit means nothing shows up in your reports. Your rankings can stay exactly where they are while business leaks away — in a place your reports can't see.

The numbers give a sense of scale, but their limits come first. A SparkToro and Similarweb clickstream study found that 68.01% of US Google browser searches in January–April 2026 produced no click at all, against 60.45% in its 2024 comparison. But that figure has three boundaries: it is US browser-panel data, excluding Google's mobile search app; the 2024 and 2026 figures came from different panels, so the change is directional rather than a precise comparison; and none of the zero-click total should be blamed on AI alone — the same study found only 0.34% of searches moved into AI Mode in that period.

Then there is the AI summary itself. A Pew Research Center study from March 2025 (68,879 Google searches by 900 US adults) found that on visits where an AI summary appeared, people clicked a traditional result in 8% of visits, compared with 15% where no summary appeared — and links cited inside the AI summary were clicked in about 1% of visits. That is a snapshot from 2025. It doesn't prove the AI summary alone caused the whole difference, and it shouldn't be treated as a 2026 global click-through rate.

## Zero-click does not mean zero business

The other side needs saying plainly: no click doesn't always mean a lost customer.

Calling straight from a Google Business Profile, requesting directions, or booking — none of those produce a website click, and all of them are real business. A client can see your number on the results page and call it directly. No visit shows up in your reports, but the phone still rings.

The real dividing line is whether the answer mentions you.

When buyers see your name, your number, and one credible line about you inside the answer, they call you. When the answer has no you in it — only your competitors — they never even open your website. Your rankings haven't moved, but the funnel has: its first half now lives inside the answer.

And one more thing worth saying clearly: fewer calls are not necessarily the AI's doing. The market, the season, your listings — all of them move call volume. Rankings can only tell you the old funnel is still there. They can't tell you where you stand in the new one. Blaming every lost call on AI is just as careless as ignoring AI entirely.
