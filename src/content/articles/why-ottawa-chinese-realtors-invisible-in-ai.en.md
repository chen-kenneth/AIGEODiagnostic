Ask AI "recommend a Chinese-speaking realtor in Ottawa" and some names come up repeatedly while others never appear. The latter is usually not bad luck — it's one of the three reasons below, or all three at once.

## 1. The Bilingual Gap: Not Existing in One Language

This is the most distinctive "invisibility" pattern for Chinese-speaking realtors.

Many realtors have plenty of Chinese-language content: WeChat public accounts, Xiaohongshu, Chinese websites. But in the English-language world, barely a trace: no English profile page, no English reviews, never mentioned by English media or local sites. The reverse is equally true — realtors who market only in English are invisible in Chinese queries.

AI organizes knowledge by language. When a buyer asks in English, it searches English sources for answers; in Chinese, Chinese sources. And Ottawa buyers use both. Covering only one language means voluntarily missing opportunities from queries in the other.

In one of our 48-query × 4-engine test rounds (anonymized), a realtor was occasionally mentioned in Chinese queries but had a zero mention rate in English queries — the AI didn't "skip" him; it simply didn't know him in the English-language world.

## 2. Missing "What Others Say": Weak Third-Party Mentions

When composing answers, AI trusts what third parties say about you more than what you say about yourself. Your website claiming "I'm a top realtor" carries far less weight than a local news article, dozens of genuine reviews on a ratings platform, or a citation on a peer's website.

Many Chinese-speaking realtors have an "island-style" online presence: a website, or a WeChat account, but almost no one else mentioning them anywhere. When AI searches and finds no cross-validating signals, it won't put the name in its answer — models prefer names they've "seen many times."

This is also why website SEO alone isn't enough: your website is you talking about yourself; AI wants others saying it too. Review management, local media coverage, industry association pages — these "other people's mouths" are the hard currency of the AI world.

## 3. Inconsistent Information: AI Can't Tell It's the Same Person

Wei Zhang is "David Zhang" on platform A, "张伟" on platform B, and platform C still lists an old phone number — with the company name written differently each time. To a human, obviously the same person. To AI, three unidentified entities whose trust scores can't accumulate in one place.

We've seen many realtors' public information with names, phone numbers, company names, and headshots written inconsistently across platforms — the majority, in fact. This is the cheapest problem to fix: unify your name, phone, company name, and photo into one identical version everywhere.

## 4. First Find Out Where You're "Invisible"

Which of the three applies to you can't be guessed. Our 48-query × 4-engine test measures exactly that: in which engines, which languages, and which question types you're invisible. Take the realtor mentioned earlier — about 10% total mention rate, all from a single engine. That's the classic "present in one engine, invisible in the rest" pattern.

Only after knowing the position can improvement begin. And improvement is long-term work: unifying information, building bilingual content, accumulating genuine reviews and third-party mentions — no shortcuts. All we can promise is accurate testing and honest gap analysis, not rankings or lead counts.
