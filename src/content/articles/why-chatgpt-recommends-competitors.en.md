## The question

This is one of the most frequently asked questions in GEO discussions. Atomic Design's collection of Reddit questions includes it verbatim: "Why does ChatGPT recommend my competitors but never me?" (Atomic Design, updated July 2026, sourced from r/SEO, r/marketing, r/bigseo and other communities). For a Chinese-speaking realtor in Ottawa, it stings: your track record and service may be no worse than your peers', yet when a buyer asks AI "who is a good Chinese-speaking realtor in Ottawa," someone else's name comes up.

## The core answer

In most cases, this is not a gap in your business. It is a **mention gap**.

When an AI builds a recommendation list, it does not first score every realtor in the city and then rank them. It synthesizes the third-party material it can read into a consensus: the names that credible, independent sources keep mentioning. If competitors appear in more independent sources — local "best realtor" roundups, industry directories, community Q&A, client reviews, media coverage — they become the consensus answer more easily, even if your actual service is just as good.

One sample illustrates how much the source mix matters. In August 2026, BaaDigi ran 511 ChatGPT answers to 88 contractor-marketing prompts: reddit.com was cited 136 times, google.com 0 times, and nothing in that sample came from a Google Business Profile (BaaDigi, updated September 2026). Note the limits of that sample: it covers US contractors, not Ottawa realtors. It shows which kinds of sources AI leans on; it does not prove that your Google Business Profile is useless. But the direction is clear: **putting information only on your own website and business profile is not enough. What matters more is what others say about you.**

One caution in the other direction: being mentioned often and closing deals well are correlated, not causally linked. A realtor who is frequently mentioned by AI did not necessarily get those deals because of AI, and closing a mention gap does not guarantee AI will recommend you. No one can promise a specific AI recommendation; any service that guarantees placement should be treated with skepticism.

## What to do

**1. Run a "source check" before changing anything.**
Take the questions buyers actually ask (for example, "Chinese-speaking realtor in Ottawa recommendation" or "who to sell with in Kanata") and ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews. Record three things: whether you are mentioned, where you appear, and which sources the answer cites. That cited-source list is your real to-do list — not a vague plan to "write more content."

**2. Fill three kinds of material, all three.**
- **Verifiable fact pages on your own site:** service areas, languages served, buyer and seller processes, contact details, written as facts that stand on their own in one sentence — not slogans. AI cites self-contained facts, not adjectives.
- **Independent third-party mentions:** local media, industry directories, "best of" roundups, genuine client reviews. The point is that others write about you, not that you write another article praising yourself.
- **Genuine community participation:** answer real questions where buyers actually ask them (for example, local community Q&A), disclose who you are, and mention yourself only when it genuinely helps. Posing as a stranger to praise yourself, or posting in bulk, gets flagged by communities — and once flagged, the trust signal is damaged too.

**3. Keep one consistent identity everywhere.**
Name spelling, brokerage affiliation, service areas, and languages served should match across your website, business profile, social accounts, and directories. If one place says Kanata, another says only Ottawa, and one uses your Chinese name while another uses your English name, it is harder for AI to stitch those fragments into one person.

**4. Judge the trend over 60–90 days, not a single answer.**
AI wording varies from answer to answer. Freeze your question set and engines, re-test on the same day each month, and watch whether your mention rate rises, holds, or falls. One day of "it didn't mention me" proves little; two consecutive months of absence from the same question set is worth a serious review.
