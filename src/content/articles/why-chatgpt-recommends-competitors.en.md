## Search is changing: from finding websites to asking AI

In the past, when consumers looked for a local service, they usually opened Google, typed in keywords, browsed the results, and compared businesses one by one.

Today, more and more consumers turn to AI tools like ChatGPT and Gemini to find information and services.

They don't necessarily want to read through a dozen websites.

They may simply ask:

- “Who are some recommended Chinese-speaking realtors in Toronto?”
- “Who are reliable mortgage brokers in Ottawa?”
- “Which immigration consultants in Canada are well regarded?”
- “Recommend a reputable renovation company in Kanata.”

AI may answer directly with a few suggestions and explain why it recommends them.

**Traditional search helps customers find answers. AI search may hand the answer to them directly.**

Both ways of searching exist side by side, but for businesses, a new kind of competition has arrived.

You used to want customers to find you in search results.

Now you also need to ask:

**When AI helps a customer look for a business like yours, does it mention you?**

## What does it mean when your competitors get recommended?

Imagine two realtors working in the same city.

Both are professional, both have years of experience, and both have good client reviews.

But when a customer asks AI for a realtor, one of them shows up in the recommended answers regularly, while the other rarely appears.

For the consumer, the first realtor gets more chances to be noticed, compared, and contacted.

The second realtor, even if equally capable, may never make it into the consumer's first round of consideration.

That doesn't mean the recommended realtor is necessarily better.

It points to a different question:

**There may be a gap between how strong your business really is and how well AI understands your brand.**

And when your competitors keep getting mentioned, that gap is worth paying attention to.

For realtors, mortgage brokers, immigration consultants, and other businesses that depend on client inquiries, every potential client contact can carry real commercial value.

## Experienced and well reviewed — so why would AI still not recommend you?

This is something many business owners overlook.

You may have been in business for years.

You may have an excellent reputation with clients.

Your service may even be more professional than some of your competitors'.

But none of that guarantees AI will fully understand you.

When AI generates a recommendation, many factors come into play: what information it can access, how the question is phrased, the search environment, and how each platform works.

So it is no contradiction for a business to be strong offline yet barely present in AI answers.

Different AI platforms may also return different recommendation lists.

**That is why what matters is not whether ChatGPT mentioned you once, but how your brand performs across a set of realistic customer questions.**

If your competitors appear often while your name is consistently absent, that is at least worth a professional check to understand where the gap is.
