A buyer in Toronto is looking for a home in Markham. Before calling anyone, they ask ChatGPT one question: "Is this agent any good?"

The AI mentions you. The name is right. Everything after it is wrong: the phone number is the one you changed three years ago, the hours are from a schedule you no longer keep, and the services list includes something you stopped offering last year. Or worse — the reviews mixed in belong to another agent with the same name.

The buyer won't call to double-check. They move on to the next name. And you never find out the conversation happened.

## Being mentioned is not the same as being introduced correctly

Appearing in an AI answer is only the first step. The few lines the answer writes about you are what the buyer actually reads.

A right name with wrong details is worse than not appearing at all. Not appearing means you missed an opportunity. Being introduced wrong means the opportunity arrived — and was walked away from, without you ever hearing a word.

## How common is this

This is not a rare glitch. The three figures below all come from published research that has been verified — but two caveats first: they are cited here from secondary summaries in industry blogs (see Sources), not from our own testing; and the samples are US local businesses or chain brands, not Chinese-speaking realtors in Canada. The scale is worth knowing. The percentages should not be pasted onto your own market.

**First, the phone number — the detail it gets wrong most.** In a study published July 23, 2026, Insites compared what ChatGPT said about 10,000 US local businesses against each business's own Google Business Profile: only 55.58% matched fully. The phone number was wrong most often, in 30.09% of results; the website was wrong in 15.59%. Some of the phone mismatches come from call-tracking numbers — numbers that do connect, but don't match the one listed. A buyer dialling the number the AI gave them can still reach the wrong place.

**Second, who gets it right.** SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index covered more than 350,000 locations belonging to 2,751 multi-location brands: Gemini was 100% accurate on business profile data, while ChatGPT and Perplexity were each 68% accurate. But the sample is chain and franchise locations, not independent agents — so "68%" must never be quoted as the realtor industry's number. The useful part is the gap itself: the assistant that read Google Maps directly was the one that got it right.

**Third, how buyers look for someone now.** BrightLocal's survey, released March 10, 2026 (1,002 US adults), found that the share of people who had used an AI tool for a local business recommendation in the past 12 months rose from 6% to 45%, with ChatGPT the most used at 31%. More and more buyers ask the AI first — and the first version the AI gives them may well be the wrong one.

## Where the AI copies it wrong from

The AI rarely invents your details out of nothing. It copies them from somewhere — from a copy that is old, wrong, or not yours at all.

**An old footer, an old number.** You changed your number and updated your website footer — but an old page, a directory listing, or an old article never got updated. The AI read the copy that didn't change.

**A service page you already took down.** The page is still live on the web, so the AI reads it as current. It has no way of knowing you stopped offering that service last year.

**A namesake.** Another agent with your name, in another city or even another country, has reviews and a transaction history that get counted as yours. You sell condos in Toronto; the AI introduces you with a bad review earned by your namesake in Vancouver.

**Stale reviews.** A three-year-old complaint is still being quoted, while the good reviews you've earned since then never got read.

**And one more, subtler cause: your own website is vague.** Your service area says "Greater Toronto Area," your specialty says "all residential." With no specifics to copy, the AI fills in the details itself. What it fills in sounds like something you would say — but you never wrote a word of it.

## Thirty seconds: ask the AI once yourself

Open ChatGPT and Gemini and ask the way a buyer would: what is your phone number, your hours, your services? Put the answers next to your own website and your Google Business Profile and compare.

Where they don't match, fix the source — your website, your Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Yelp. There is no public way to correct an AI answer directly; you can only fix the material it reads. And if you fix one place and leave the old copy elsewhere, the AI will keep finding the old one next time.
