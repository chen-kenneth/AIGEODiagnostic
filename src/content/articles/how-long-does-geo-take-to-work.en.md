You just signed up for GEO — or you are about to. The question in your head is simple enough: "I did the work, so when will I start seeing myself in AI answers?"

Here is the honest answer up front: **there is no single number.** That is not a dodge. "Seeing results" is actually several different things, and each one runs on its own clock. Before asking "how long," you have to ask which one you mean.

## The first thing: a new page getting cited — weeks

This is the fastest layer to move.

You publish a new page that answers a question buyers actually ask. Once the crawlers reach it, AI answers to that question can start citing it. The agency scaile puts it this way on its public page: crawlers pick up a change within days to weeks, and a new page can be cited within weeks.

But being cited once is not the same as holding the spot. Cited today, gone next week when the question is phrased differently. A single citation is a signal, not a result.

## The second thing: your share of mentions — months

What actually tells you something is a different layer: across a fixed set of buyer questions, how often are you mentioned?

Take questions like "recommend a Chinese-speaking realtor in Ottawa" or "who is a good Chinese realtor for buying in Markham." Ask the same set every month across several AI engines, and count how often you show up. That share moves month to month, because it takes many pages getting better together — each one answering one question — before AI starts treating you as a regular answer for that kind of question.

Three GEO firms say roughly the same thing on their public pages. All of it is guidance, not a promise:

- Crescendo: run an initial 90-day programme — baseline, priority fixes, re-test — and extend the assessment to three to six months.
- Kaliber: initial visibility shows up in 4–8 weeks for branded queries; compounding visibility across non-branded buyer questions typically takes 3–6 months.
- scaile: the share of AI answers that name you moves over months.

Put together, it comes down to one line: **three months is enough to judge whether the work is happening; six to twelve months is what it takes to see whether the trend is real.** Nothing moving after three months means something needs investigating. Calling it done after three months is too early.

## The promise nobody can make

One more thing has to be said up front: **nobody controls what AI platforms recommend.**

A "results in two weeks" promise cannot be given. Anyone giving it is either talking about one change to their own website, or showing you one lucky screenshot — the one time you were there, when asking the same question differently the next day might show nothing.

So before you sign, the question is not "how long." It is these three:

1. Is there a baseline — what do I actually look like in AI answers today, before we start?
2. What moves first, and what moves later — what is the order, and which layer comes first?
3. How often do we re-test — what are we comparing against, and what proves it moved or did not?

If a provider cannot answer those three, do not believe any date they quote.
