"Isn't GEO just SEO with a new name?" It's the question we hear most. The answer is clear: no. They're two different systems that read different signals and optimize for different goals.

## 1. User Behavior Has Changed

Consider two data points. Forrester's *2026 Buyers' Journey Survey* (about 18,000 global enterprise buyers) found 94% used generative AI in their most recent purchase process, up from 89% the year before. SparkToro's research based on Similarweb clickstream data found that roughly 68% of Google searches in the US ended without a click in January–April 2026, up from 58.5% in 2024. (Note: zero-click has many causes, including featured snippets and knowledge panels; AI-generated answers are the fastest-growing new driver — but 68% is not the share of AI search.)

Translation: more and more people get answers directly inside AI responses instead of opening webpages. SEO fights for "rankings"; GEO fights for "being written into the answer." These are two completely different games.

## 2. Two Systems, Different Signals

Search engines do **ranking**: ordering webpages into a list. They read signals like backlink counts, keywords, and technical site health.

Generative AI does **synthesis**: it retrieves a set of sources, then fuses the information into a direct answer. It reads different signals — how often you're mentioned across the web, whether your information is consistent across platforms, whether authoritative sources have mentioned you, and whether your content is structured for easy extraction.

Researchers including a Princeton-affiliated team published "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization" at ACM KDD 2024 (arXiv:2311.09735), benchmarking roughly 10,000 queries. They found that adding statistics, citing reliable sources, and including expert quotes can improve content visibility in generative answers — with "adding statistics" alone delivering about a 41% visibility lift in the main experiment. Keyword stuffing, meanwhile, is nearly useless in generative engines and can even backfire.

## 3. Ranking ≠ Being Cited

This is the most counterintuitive point: the #1 Google result isn't necessarily cited by AI, and AI-cited sources may come from deep in the results. An Ahrefs study from March 2026 (863,000 keywords, ~4 million AI citations) found that only 37.9% of cited sources ranked in Google's top 10; 31.2% ranked 11–100; and 31.0% weren't in the top 100 at all.

The reason isn't hard to see: search engines rank by "page quality"; AI selects by "what the answer needs." It wants a citable fact that directly supports the answer — not a well-ranked homepage.

## 4. What It Means for Realtors

Three points:

First, **a well-SEO'd website doesn't mean AI will mention you**. These are two different exams with different passing bars.

Second, the materials needed to "be written into the answer" differ from those needed to "rank #1": real transaction data, a clear service description, reviews and mentions on third-party platforms, consistent basic information across platforms — that's how AI "knows" you.

Third, **do both languages**. A realtor with presence only in the Chinese-language world is nearly invisible to AI in English queries, and vice versa. Chinese buyers in Canada use both.

## 5. How They Relate

SEO is the foundation; GEO is a new layer on top. A shaky foundation (a site that won't load, messy information) means nothing built on it works; but guarding only the foundation in the AI era means missing more and more customers who "never open a webpage."

One caveat: GEO improves your chances of being mentioned and cited by AI. No one can promise rankings, recommendations, or a specific number of leads. Anyone who promises those — including us — deserves skepticism.
