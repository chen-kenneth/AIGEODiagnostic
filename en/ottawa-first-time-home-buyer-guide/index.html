Buying Your First Home in Ottawa

Buying your first home in Ottawa usually starts with mortgage pre-approval, followed by choosing neighbourhoods, viewing homes, making an offer, completing conditions and inspection, and closing with a lawyer.

Step 1 — Mortgage pre-approval

Before viewing a single home, talk to a bank or mortgage broker. Pre-approval tells you the maximum you can borrow, locks a rate for a set period (commonly around 120 days), and signals to sellers that your offer is serious. Bring: proof of income, employment letter, bank statements, ID.

Step 2 — Set your budget

Your budget is not just the purchase price. Add land transfer tax, legal fees, moving costs, and a buffer for immediate repairs. In Canada the minimum down payment is 5% on the first $500,000 and 10% on the portion above that, up to 20% — below 20%, mortgage default insurance is generally required, which adds to your cost.

Step 3 — Choose your area

Narrow to 2–3 neighbourhoods before heavy viewing. Weigh commute, schools, amenities and price per square foot. In Ottawa's west end, Kanata, Stittsville and Nepean each suit different budgets and lifestyles, while Barrhaven in the south is a favourite with growing families — visit at different times of day before committing.

Step 4 — View properties

See enough homes to calibrate: after 8–12 viewings most buyers know what "good value" looks like in their segment. Look past staging at structure, layout, natural light and the big-ticket items (roof, furnace, windows, foundation).

Step 5 — Offer strategy

An offer states price, deposit, conditions, inclusions/exclusions and closing date. Strategy depends on the market: in competitive situations, fewer conditions and flexible closing dates strengthen an offer; in balanced markets, you can negotiate harder on price and terms.

Step 6 — Conditions

Conditions protect you. The two most common for first-time buyers:

  • Financing condition — a window (often 5–10 business days) to secure final mortgage approval.
  • Home inspection condition — a professional inspection; major issues let you renegotiate or walk away.

When all conditions are met or waived, the deal becomes firm.

Step 7 — Inspection

A licensed home inspector examines structure, roof, plumbing, electrical, heating and moisture. Attend if you can — the walkthrough teaches you more about the house than the report alone.

Step 8 — Lawyer and closing

In Ontario, a real estate lawyer handles closing: title search, property tax adjustments, mortgage fund transfer and ownership registration. Closing commonly happens 30–90 days after offer acceptance. Your lawyer provides a final statement of funds before closing day.

Typical costs beyond the price

  • Land transfer tax (Ontario + City of Ottawa each levy one; first-time buyers may qualify for rebates — check current rules)
  • Legal fees and disbursements
  • Home inspection fee
  • Moving costs
  • Immediate repairs and setup buffer

Common first-time buyer mistakes

  • Skipping pre-approval and falling for a home outside budget
  • Waiving the inspection to "win" — then inheriting a $20,000 roof
  • Budgeting only the down payment, forgetting closing costs
  • Choosing the area after the house instead of before
  • Making major credit changes (new car loan, job switch) mid-transaction

Ottawa neighbourhood considerations

Ottawa is a city of distinct pockets. Downtown and central neighbourhoods offer walkability at higher prices; the west end (Kanata, Stittsville, Nepean) offers newer stock, good schools and tech-employment proximity, and the south (Barrhaven) offers family-friendly newer communities. Match the area to your daily life, not just the listing photos.

FAQ

What is the first step to buying a home in Ottawa?

Get a mortgage pre-approval. It tells you what you can borrow and shows sellers you are a serious buyer.

How much down payment do first-time buyers need in Ottawa?

The minimum ranges from 5% on the first $500,000 to 10% above that, up to 20%. Below 20%, mortgage default insurance is generally required.

What conditions should a first-time buyer include in an offer?

Financing and home inspection are the standard protective conditions — each gives you a defined window to verify before the deal becomes firm.

Who handles the closing of a home purchase in Ontario?

A real estate lawyer: title search, tax adjustments, fund transfers and registration of ownership.

How long does buying a first home in Ottawa usually take?

Searching takes weeks to months; after offer acceptance, closing is commonly 30–90 days later.
